What drives the price

  • Slab vs prehung: a slab is fitted to the frame you already have; a prehung door comes in its own jamb, so the old jamb comes out and the new one is set plumb in the rough opening.
  • Out-of-square frames: pre-war openings settle and lean, so a slab is scribed to the old frame or a prehung jamb is shimmed further. An oversized or out-of-square storm-door opening is its own tier.
  • Floor clearance: new flooring, a threshold or a thick rug can mean trimming the bottom of the slab.
  • What the hardware bolts into: a barn door track goes into studs, or onto a header board first over plaster or masonry; pocket doors are priced on an existing pocket frame.
  • Quantity: on the slab, prehung, closet and pocket lines, two or three doors in one visit are one tier, so label each door and opening before the visit.
  • Building rules: co-op and condo COI requirements, permitted work hours and a service-elevator booking for the door delivery decide when the job can be scheduled.

What's included

  • Measuring the opening and checking the frame, swing direction and floor clearance
  • Hanging the door, with hinges mortised and the latch and strike fitted
  • Trimming, boring and new casing in the tiers that name them
  • A swing and latch test so the door closes without scraping or springing open

What's quoted separately

  • The door, jamb, casing, hinges and lockset — the parts themselves
  • Painting, staining and finish trim
  • Repairs to a loose or rotted jamb, or correcting an uneven floor — flagged with the adjustment needed before booking

When it's not a handyman job

  • Cutting a new opening, removing or re-framing studs, and anything at DOB permit level goes to a licensed contractor. A pocket-door frame kit is stud framing too, so your contractor builds the frame and we hang the slab and fit the track and hardware once it is in.

Frequently asked questions

Is a prehung door more expensive to install than a slab?

Usually, yes. A slab reuses the frame you already have, so the work is fitting the door to it: trimming, mortising the hinges and boring for the lockset. A prehung door arrives in its own jamb, so the old jamb comes out, the new one is shimmed plumb and level in the opening and the casing is reset. When the existing frame is sound and square, a slab is the cheaper route; when the frame is damaged or badly out of square, prehung is usually the better one.

My door frame is out of square. Will a new door fit?

Usually — few pre-war NYC frames are perfectly square. A slab can be scribed and planed to the frame's actual shape, and a prehung jamb is shimmed until it hangs plumb. Send a photo of the closed door showing the gaps along the top and sides, and the width and height measured at both ends of the opening; that shows which route costs less. A loose or rotted jamb, or a floor that slopes, is flagged with the adjustment needed before we book.

How does a co-op or condo building change a door installation?

Mostly through paperwork and timing. Many buildings ask for a Certificate of Insurance naming the management company, allow work only on weekdays within set hours, and want the service elevator reserved for the door delivery. Repair ASAP LLC provides the COI at no additional cost; send the building's rules with your photos so the visit is booked inside them. The apartment entry door onto the public hallway is typically fire-rated, with building and code limits, so replacing it needs management sign-off before you order anything; lock changes on it are covered in the lock installation guide.

Can you hang a barn door on a plaster or brick wall?

Yes. The track carries the whole weight of the door, so it has to bolt into studs. On pre-war plaster or masonry, where the studs are not where the track needs them, a header board is fixed to the wall first and the track mounts to it — that is the middle barn-door tier in the table. Double doors or an oversized, heavy slab are the top tier.

Do you install pocket doors?

On an existing pocket frame, yes: re-hanging the slab, or replacing the track, rollers and hardware. Building a new pocket into the wall is stud framing, so the frame kit goes to your contractor, and we hang the slab and fit the track and hardware once the frame is in.

Can you replace an exterior entry door or add a storm door on a house?

Yes. In our Staten Island entry door case study, the old storm door, the door and the worn frame came out, a new prehung exterior door was set plumb, level and square, the unused second lock bore was capped and the perimeter was sealed with low-expansion spray foam, in about three and a half hours; a new storm door with an automatic closer then took about four hours, most of it lock and closer prep. An exterior entry door is quoted from photos of both sides of the opening. For storm doors, a direct swap into an existing storm-door frame is the first storm line in the table, and a new storm door that has to be drilled, shimmed and sealed with a closer is the next.

What should I send for a door installation quote?

Photos of the opening and the existing door from both sides, the width and height of the opening, the swing direction, the door type or a product link, a photo of the hinges and latch, and your building's COI or work-hour rules. If you are replacing several doors, label each door and opening — sizes that look identical often are not.
